# Using CrateCompass

https://cratecompass.vercel.app

CrateCompass helps you find music by showing its work. Every connection names
the source that reported it, every factual answer cites the records behind it,
and when the data does not support an answer, it says so.

---

## Getting started

Create an account with an email and password. You can browse discovery and
discographies signed out; saving, asking questions, and creating playlists need
an account.

**Connecting Spotify is optional.** Discovery, explanations, discographies and
Q&A all work without it. Connect only if you want playlists created in your
Spotify account.

---

## Finding related artists

Go to **Discover** and search for an artist you already trust.

Search returns *canonical candidates* rather than jumping to a guess. Pick the
right one — this matters for artists sharing a name, and the disambiguation text
is there to help. Once confirmed, you get a ranked list of related artists.

Each result shows:

- **The provider's rank.** ListenBrainz's own position, never renumbered.
- **A strength badge**, relative to the strongest match in this set. Similarity
  scores mean little in isolation, so they are expressed relative to what else
  came back.
- **What MusicBrainz calls the artist**, with any disambiguation.

Everything costing another provider request — the evidence, the written
explanation, the Spotify link — sits behind a button, so a page of twelve
results does not fire fifty requests you did not ask for.

### Why this match?

Expand it and you get two things, in this order:

1. **Evidence** — what the providers actually reported. Shared tags, similarity
   position, and so on, each labelled with its source.
2. **A reading of the evidence** — the interpretation.

That order is deliberate. Facts first, interpretation second, clearly separated.
The reading is generated by a language model that is required to cite the
evidence it used; output that cites something it was not given is discarded and
replaced with a plain restatement of the facts.

You can add what *you* like about the seed artist and have the reading rewritten
around it. Only your own words are sent. Nothing from Spotify is ever included.

**Save** keeps the artist and the explanation that convinced you. **Dismiss**
removes a result, and can be undone.

## Describing a mood

Go to **Mood** and write a few sentences — the room, the weather, the pace, what
you do *not* want. Plain language works better than keywords.

The steps are:

1. **Interpretation.** Your words become structured criteria you can read.
2. **Seed confirmation.** You approve the artists it proposes starting from.
   This step exists because tag search ranks by text relevance, not quality, and
   occasionally proposes something absurd. You catch it, not the machine.
3. **Building.** Tracks are gathered. This is the slow part, and the interface
   says why: MusicBrainz allows one request per second.
4. **Review.** Edit and reorder before anything is created.
5. **Approval.** Only then is a Spotify playlist created.

Nothing reaches your Spotify account until you approve it.

**About track choice, honestly:** tracks are the opening tracks of an artist's
studio releases, not their best-known songs. The listening-data source that
would rank them is currently disabled upstream. The interface names the album
each track came from rather than implying a ranking it does not have.

## Reading a discography

Every artist page shows the full release-group timeline from MusicBrainz —
albums, EPs, singles, live records, compilations — with dates at the precision
actually recorded. `1994` and `1994-08-22` are different claims, and both are
shown as they are.

For very prolific artists, retrieval can hit a safety bound. When it does, the
page says the list is incomplete rather than presenting a partial list as
whole.

### Asking questions

The Q&A panel takes factual questions about releases. Suggested questions are
derived from the records actually retrieved, so you should not be offered a
question these records cannot answer.

Answers cite the releases they used, with links. Three things worth knowing:

- **"The records do not say" is a real answer**, not a failure. It is what you
  get instead of a plausible sentence.
- **Counting questions are withheld when retrieval was incomplete**, because a
  count over a partial list is a confident wrong answer.
- **Opinions are not answered from records.** It answers what the data supports.

Your daily AI allowance is shown next to the button.

## Library and history

**Library** holds what you saved, with the explanation that accompanied it,
stored as it was at the time. It does not re-ask a model later — you would get a
different reason than the one you kept.

Search it, filter by where an item came from or by tag, sort it, add notes.
Removing an item offers an undo; the restored copy is a new entry dated today,
and the interface says so. Bulk removal states its real count and offers no
undo.

**History** records what you actually did: what was asked, what was selected,
which provider answered, the outcome, and when.

If your account predates the feature, history says it began on a date rather
than implying nothing happened. Nothing was backfilled, because reconstructing
events would put plausible timestamps on things never observed.

## Settings and your data

**Provider readiness** shows what is actually configured, read from the live
environment.

**Disconnect Spotify** removes the stored connection. Playlists already created
stay in your Spotify account — they are yours.

**Delete account** removes your identity and cascades to every record attached
to it. It needs your password and a typed confirmation. Deletion is genuine:
there is no hidden flag or holding table.

For a copy of your data, contact the operator. A self-service export does not
exist yet.

## When things go wrong

The service depends on several third-party providers, and it tries to tell you
which one is having trouble rather than showing a generic error.

| You see | It means |
| --- | --- |
| "MusicBrainz is unavailable" | The metadata source is not responding. Try later. |
| A partial-result warning | Some data arrived, some did not. What is shown is real; it is not everything. |
| "You have reached your daily limit" | The AI allowance resets daily. Browsing and discographies still work. |
| "Reconnect Spotify" | The connection expired or permissions changed. |
| "The records do not say" | Not an error. The data does not support an answer. |

The service will not invent data to fill a gap. An empty or partial result is
reported as exactly that.

## Accessibility

Fully keyboard navigable, with a skip link and visible focus indicators. Screen
readers get status announcements for results, saves and progress. Honours
`prefers-reduced-motion` — animations are not applied at all, rather than merely
shortened. Tested to WCAG 2.2 AA with no known violations, and reflows without
horizontal scrolling from 320px upward.

If you hit an accessibility barrier, that is a bug worth reporting.
